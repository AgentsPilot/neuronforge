One answer decides the rest of the flow

Step 2 is the only branch in the whole editor. Two of the three paths end there, which is why asking it early is worth more than asking it well.

It is free

Two questions, then publish

  1. What it is
  2. It is free
  3. How you get paid
  4. Publish

Asks for

Your working hours, and nothing else. No invoice details, no card account.

Free and no appointment is refused: nothing is held and nothing is charged, so there is nothing for a client to book.

I charge a set price

The only path with a third step

  1. What it is
  2. $300, paid in full
  3. Card, or you invoice
  4. Publish

Asks for

Hours, then your invoice details, then a card account if they pay by card.

Invoice details come before Stripe. With no card account the service is invoiced, so the paperwork is what makes it work at all.

I price it per job

No price, and no card question

  1. What it is
  2. Priced per job
  3. How you get paid
  4. Publish

Asks for

Your invoice details, because accepting a quote raises a real invoice. Never a card account: what a quote collects is decided in the quote.

The client requests, you send a quote, they accept, the invoice follows. The booking can happen before any of that.

Why this is the right cut: it is the model the code already holds. serviceRequirements asks whether it is quoted, then whether money moves, then how it is collected — these three branches exactly. The editor is the only layer that flattened them into one row of chips.
